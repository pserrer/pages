Privacy Policy

Nearstead · Last updated 23 November 2026

This policy describes how Nearstead, the Android app with package name com.pserrer.nearstead, handles information.

In short. Your diary, people, notes and contact history stay on your phone. Nearstead has no app account, no cloud diary sync, no ads, no analytics and no tracking. We do not sell your data. The optional online services are described below.

What stays on your phone

  • People and notes. Nearstead stores the people you add, your notes, manually logged contacts and diary entries in an encrypted database (SQLCipher). The encryption key is generated on your device and protected by Android’s Keystore.
  • Contacts. With permission, Nearstead reads names, phone numbers, email addresses and birthdays for the people you link. It displays address-book photos without copying them and can import newly added numbers or email addresses for linked contacts. If you enable Details from your contacts, it can also import related people, dates, company, job title and town, without reading the street address for this feature. Nearstead does not write to your address book.
  • Phone calls. After a call you started through Nearstead, you can return to the app and confirm it for your history.
  • Calendar. With permission, Nearstead reads event times and attendee email addresses in the calendars you select to match meetings to people you know. It does not read event titles, descriptions or locations. A meeting enters your contact history when you confirm it.
  • Photos. With permission, Nearstead reads accessible photos and their available date and location information to show them on diary days. The images stay in your photo library. Turning off the photo source clears Nearstead’s local photo index.
  • Places and routes. If you enable the Places source and grant location access, Android records locations, including in the background, to reconstruct visits and routes for your diary. Pending location samples are encrypted before they are imported into your diary.
  • Music and podcasts. If enabled and notification access is granted, Nearstead reads playback information such as titles and artists from supported Android media sessions to record what you listened to. It does not record the audio.
  • Voice input. Dictation uses Android’s on-device speech recognition where offline recognition is available. The resulting text is shown for you to review before saving.

Message notifications and on-device AI

When you enable notification access, Nearstead can record who contacted you and when from notifications in apps you choose. Message text is used for suggestions only if you separately turn on Suggest from messages. This covers incoming one-to-one messages. The text is held on the phone until Nearstead next opens and looks for something worth remembering. The original text is then discarded. Suggestions you do not act on expire after two weeks and are excluded from backups. Turning the feature off clears pending suggestions. A suggestion becomes a saved memory only if you accept it.

If you enable Suggestions on the phone and your device supports it, Android AICore’s Gemini Nano model processes inputs on the device. Depending on the feature, it can suggest a memory, tidy dictated text, suggest a reminder, describe a diary photo, or write a recap from locally recorded activity. Nearstead does not send those inputs to a cloud AI service. Suggestions that add to your notes or reminders need your confirmation.

When Nearstead uses online services

  • Maps, place names and weather. A map requests tiles for the displayed area from the configured map provider, or OpenFreeMap by default. Place searches send the name you enter to the search service. Optional place naming uses Android’s geocoder, which may send coordinates to a network service. When weather is enabled and available, Nearstead sends rounded coordinates to Open-Meteo to retrieve weather for the diary.
  • App updates. Nearstead uses Expo Application Services (EAS Update) when it checks for and downloads updates. The update service receives technical information such as the app version, a random install identifier and the IP address of the request. Your diary and contact history are not included. See Expo’s privacy policy.
  • Purchases. Google Play Billing processes Nearstead purchases, restores them and supplies purchase status. Nearstead keeps your entitlement on the device. Google handles your account and payment information under Google’s privacy policy.
  • Opening another app. When you choose to call, message, or open a search or other link, Nearstead passes the information needed for that action to the app or service you open. A prefilled message is sent only when you send it yourself.

Your choices, backups and deletion

You can turn optional sources and suggestions off in Nearstead or revoke permissions in Android settings. You can delete diary entries, notes and people from the app. When a person is removed, their saved history and notes are removed after the undo period.

Android cloud backup is disabled for Nearstead. A backup is created only when you ask for one, encrypted with your passphrase, and saved or shared to the destination you choose. Clearing app storage or uninstalling Nearstead removes the local app data. Backup files and exports you saved elsewhere can be deleted at their destination.

You can also enable Lock Nearstead. Android verifies your fingerprint, face or screen lock. Nearstead does not receive or store biometric data or your screen-lock credentials.
